Title

A Visual Inquiry into Appointments During the First 300 Days of the Second Trump Administration

Purpose

Political appointees hold immense power in the American government. Unlike most democracies, senior positions across the executive branch change after a new president takes office. These positions range from Department Secretaries to ambassadors to legal counsels, and they are selected based on merit, experience, and, increasingly, political leanings and loyalty to the executive (Universiy of Chicago Center for Effective Management).

As students studying government, we were alarmed and curious about the increasing attention paid to political appointees over the past year. Appointees have faced controversy during their appointment process over their character and qualifications, and it seems like appointees who take office end up getting fired or quitting. Studies have show that vacancies and incompetent appointees produce less effective administration, limiting government responsiveness and sevice towards citizens. Understanding trends in nominations and appointment during Trump's first 300 days in office helps to better understand the political landscape for the ret of his term.

Research Questions

  1. What are the typical ethnic and gender demographics of Trump's political appointees?
  2. How do these demographics compare to those of other recent presidents?
  3. How has the appointment process changed under Trump?

Research Background

Our first and second research question delves into the gender and ethnicity of Trump's candidates. Diversity is important to having an effective and representative government. Trump has publicly criticized diversity, equity, and inclusion . We aim to quantify the scale of his efforts to reduce DEI initiatives by comparing the gender and ethncity of his nominees to past presidents' nominees.

Our third research question explores the appointment process under Trump. The Senate operates as a check on executive power and must approve all nominations. Increased polarization, however, has slowed down the appointment process. As we created this project, the Senate also approved a rule change that allowed for nominations to be approved with a simple majority rather than the previous 60-vote threshold. We want to explore whether these political changes have affected the appointment process under Trump compared to past presidents.
